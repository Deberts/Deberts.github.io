Game rules

Deberts has many rule variants. The table settings determine the number of players, target score (301, 501 or 1001), team play and whether Bella requires winning a trick. Local games have no move timer; online games allow 30, 45 or 60 seconds per move.

Cards and points

The game uses 32 cards: seven through ace in each suit.
Non-trump cards, strongest first: ace (11), ten (10), king (4), queen (3), jack (2), nine (0), eight (0), seven (0).
Trump cards, strongest first: jack (20), nine (14), ace (11), ten (10), king (4), queen (3), eight (0), seven (0).
The last trick is worth an additional 10 points.

Declarations

Tierce: three consecutive cards in the same suit, worth 20 points.
Deberts: four consecutive cards in the same suit, worth 50 points.
Bella: the king and queen of trump, worth 20 points.

Tierce and deberts are declared during the first round of tricks. Declarations are compared by their highest card. Deberts beats tierce. For otherwise equal declarations, trump wins; if neither is trump, the first declaration in turn order wins. A stronger opposing declaration cancels the weaker declarations. You must win at least one trick for declarations to count toward your score.

Bella is declared when playing the king or queen of trump. The table setting controls whether winning the trick is required for Bella to count.

Choosing trump

Turns proceed clockwise. Players initially receive six cards each. The remaining deck is placed in the middle, with a face-up card showing the proposed trump suit.
During the first bidding round, each player may accept the proposed suit or pass. In the second round, a player may choose any other suit. If everyone passes, the player with the forced bid must choose trump.
Once trump is chosen, players receive three more cards each. The declarer leads the first trick.

Playing a trick

Follow the lead suit when possible. If you cannot follow suit, play trump when required; if you have neither the lead suit nor trump, play another card. When playing trump, you must beat the current winning trump when possible.
Trump beats any non-trump card. Otherwise, the strongest card in the lead suit wins the trick. The trick winner leads the next trick.

Scoring

At the end of the hand, card points, valid declarations and the last-trick bonus are counted. A player who takes no tricks receives a penalty of 100 points. A declarer who loses the hand receives a bait; three baits also incur a 100-point penalty.
The next player takes the forced-bid position for the next hand. Continue playing until the target score is reached. In a team game, partners share a score.
